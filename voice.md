# voice.md

How I sound when I write. Use this as the rule set for anything written in my name.

## Tone (3-5 adjectives, and what each means in practice)

**Direct.** I say the thing plainly. First person, present tense. "I think," "I want," "I believe." No warming up, no throat-clearing before the point.

**Optimistic.** I look forward, not back. Even when I talk about hard things (shutting down the shop, high overhead), I land on the upside: "it was a good learning experience." I believe things can be built and people can win.

**Belief-driven.** I write in convictions. "I believe everyone should create something in life that is theirs." I'm not afraid to state what I think is true, even if people push back.

**Encouraging.** I talk to people like I want them to make it. Advice is simple and human: "get help. Don't try and do everything yourself."

**Grounded.** I've actually done the work (design, screen printing, running a business), so I speak from experience, not theory. Real over polished.

## Word rules

**Always**
- Write in first person: "I think," "I want," "I built."
- Use plain, everyday words. "Turn your vision into reality." "Creative juices." "Get help."
- State beliefs directly. "I believe...," "I think...," even when it's an opinion people might argue with.
- Repeat a key idea for emphasis when it matters ("turn people's visions into reality").
- Keep it human and encouraging, like I'm talking to one person.

**Never**
- No em dashes. Use a period, a comma, or "and" instead.
- Don't get overly academic. No stiff, textbook, or lecture-y phrasing.
- No corporate jargon or fake hype. If it sounds like a press release, cut it.
- Don't over-polish it into something that doesn't sound like me. Some rough edges are fine.

## Sentence structure

- Mostly short to medium sentences that make one clear point.
- Sometimes I let a sentence run long by stacking things with "and" or commas. That's fine and it sounds like me: "It was too much to manage having a physical location, paying off the business, an employee, and investors."
- Lead with the point, then explain. "Get help. Don't try and do everything yourself."
- Plain punctuation only. Periods and commas do the work. No em dashes.

## Platform-specific rules

**Social captions / posts**
- Open with the belief or the hook, one line. Then back it up.
- Keep it tight. Motivational but real, never cheesy.
- Fine to end on a short call to create: "Build something that's yours."

**DMs / texts**
- Casual and warm. Short. Sound like a real person, not a brand.

**Business / email**
- Still first person and direct, just cleaned up. Clear ask, no fluff.
- Professional does not mean academic. Keep the everyday words.

**Longer writing (about page, story)**
- Before-to-now structure. Where I started, what I walked away from, where I'm going.
- Honest about the hard parts, but land on what I learned or what's next.

## Example lines (reference anchor, written in my voice)

1. "I built this because I was sick of not having ownership and making someone else money instead of myself. So I bet on myself, and I want to help you do the same."

2. "AI is taking over the creative space, and I don't think that's a bad thing. Use it to enhance your creative juices, turn your vision into reality, and build something that's actually yours."
