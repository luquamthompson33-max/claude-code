# audience.md

Who I'm actually talking to, in their own words. Two businesses, two audiences. Built from my own answers plus real language pulled from public reviews, surveys, and community posts (sources at the bottom).

Both audiences live mostly on **Instagram and Facebook**. Pricing is custom per project.

---

## Business 1 — Create Your Utopia (CYU)

### Who they are
Entrepreneurs, business owners, creatives, and artists who have a vision but need help making it real. They need design work, art, video, clothing, websites, AI consulting, and AI automation. The through-line: they want their brand and their business to grow, and they want someone to turn their vision into reality.

**The core problem I solve:** brand presence and business growth through branding, marketing, and automations.

### Pain points (what they're actually dealing with)
- **No time.** Lack of time is the #1 marketing frustration for small business owners, cited by **73%** in Buffer's 2025 survey. **64%** say social media is their most frustrating marketing activity.
- **Posting into the void.** They "post in short bursts when they have time, disappear for a bit, then feel guilty and try to make up for it," and that inconsistency hurts their reach and confidence.
- **Effort with nothing to show for it.** They're "staying up late designing flyers, posting whenever they get a free moment, even running ads, and still feel like nothing is happening."
- **Fragmented, no strategy.** They've got "fragmented pieces of collateral like websites, newsletters, email marketing, and social media posts but no sound strategy" tying it together.
- **Can't afford an agency, don't know who to trust.** Branding agencies "typically charge $5,000 to $50,000 for a small business engagement." When a quote seems low, they worry "the conversation feels rushed or superficial."
- **The brand doesn't match the work.** "Clients associate the quality of your work with how the brand is perceived." A personal Gmail or a thrown-together logo makes them "look like a hobbyist rather than a serious professional."

### Fears
- That they'll waste money on branding and still not look legit.
- That they're working hard and getting nowhere ("99% of small businesses fail to get results from their social media content").
- That customers won't trust them ("81% of consumers say they need to trust a brand before they will buy from it").
- That they'll never look as credible as the bigger players.

### Dreams / goals
- To finally **look professional and be taken seriously**. "If your branding is tight, your materials are polished... people will assume your business is well-run."
- To have a brand that's **consistent everywhere** so the whole thing feels like one real company.
- To grow the business without living inside their marketing 24/7 (this is exactly where automations come in).
- To turn the vision in their head into something real and out in the world.

### The language that repeats
- "I don't have time."
- "I have no idea what I'm doing."
- "Where do I even start?"
- "I want to look professional / be taken seriously."
- "I can't afford $5k–$50k for an agency."
- "I'm working so hard and nothing is happening."
- "I need branding, marketing, and a website but it's overwhelming."

### How to speak to them (CYU)
Lead with **their vision and their growth**, not my tools. Speak to the overwhelm and the time crunch, then position branding + marketing + automation as the way to look legit and grow without doing it all themselves. "Turn your vision into reality" lands because it's literally what they're searching for. Show, don't claim: proof of professional-looking work does more than adjectives.

## Business 2 — Dumb Jocks

### Who they are
Athletes, sports fans, and people into streetwear. They want cool gear they can wear that connects them to something and represents the brand and the culture.

**The core problem I solve:** creating cool gear people actually want to wear, gear that connects and represents.

### Pain points (what they're actually dealing with)
- **Cheap gear that falls apart.** Streetwear reviews repeatedly split on quality: fans love the pieces, but the complaints are "poor quality... manufacturing defects, incorrect sizing, and thin material." Quality is the number one thing they talk about.
- **Sizing and fit misses.** A constant across apparel reviews: "sizing issues or items that didn't meet expectations."
- **Generic gear that says nothing.** They don't just want a shirt, they want something that means something and reps who they are and what they're part of.

### Fears
- Paying for gear that shows up thin, off-size, or nothing like the photos.
- Wearing something generic that doesn't represent them.
- A brand that looks cool but doesn't actually deliver.

### Dreams / goals
- **Gear that reps them.** "The clothes you choose to wear send a message about who you are and what you stand for."
- **Confidence.** "When you wear something that aligns with your identity, you feel more confident stepping into a room." When they wear something they love, they "carry themselves differently, more confidently, more authentically."
- **Belonging.** "Wearing a particular brand can communicate shared values, a lifestyle, or a community." For teams and fans, gear that "best represents their team, its tradition, and its community."
- **Compliments.** The best reviews are people saying they "receive compliments every time they wear it."

### The language that repeats
- "It reps me / it represents who I am."
- "I get compliments every time I wear it."
- "It makes me feel confident."
- "The quality is actually good" (or the opposite: "thin material, fell apart").
- "It fits perfect" (or "the sizing was off").
- "It's part of the culture / the community."

### How to speak to them (Dumb Jocks)
Sell **identity and quality**, in that order. The gear reps them, the team, the culture. Back it up on quality and fit, because that's the make-or-break in every review. Speak the language of confidence and belonging. Athletes and fans want to wear something that connects them to the crew and gets noticed.

## Shared truths across both audiences
- Both live on **Instagram and Facebook**. Visual-first. Show the work, show the gear, show real people.
- Both are buying a **feeling**: CYU clients want to feel legit and in control; Dumb Jocks customers want to feel confident and connected.
- Both have been **burned by cheap or generic** before (bad branding / bad gear). Trust and proof matter more than hype.
- My promise fits both: **turn your vision into reality**, and make it something that's actually yours.
